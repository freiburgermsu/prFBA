# Frameworks for improved closest-hit 16S mapping — beyond NT-v2 cosine

## 1. Executive answer

Your measured failure is not a *retrieval* problem — cosine already recalls the true #1 in its top-100 for ~92% of ASVs — it is a **fine-ordering/metric problem inside a dense, near-identical V4-V5 cloud**: a single mean-pooled vector cannot resolve sequences that differ by a few nucleotides, so cosine ordering diverges from exact %identity (top-1 match 54.7%, Spearman 0.55). The single best fix is therefore **not a new embedding but a two-stage retrieve-then-rerank pipeline: keep NT-v2 cosine as the recaller, then re-order the top-k with exhaustive optimal global alignment — VSEARCH `usearch_global --maxaccepts 0 --maxrejects 0`** — which *is* the %identity definition your ground truth uses and makes ordering exact by construction, in milliseconds-to-sub-second per query (your 357 s/query figure is full-DB *un-indexed* alignment and is ~3-4 orders of magnitude too pessimistic at this scale). The top alternatives, in order: (2) a **learned %identity regressor (Identity/FASTCAR)** as an even-faster stage-2 reranker that correlates 0.97 with Needleman-Wunsch; (3) **contrastive/metric-learning fine-tuning** of a single-nucleotide backbone that distills your own alignment %identity as the target (Scorpio/DNABERT-S recipe) if you must keep a single-vector retriever; and (4) for the *taxonomy/novelty* deliverable specifically, **phylogenetic placement (DEPP/C-DEPP, EPA-ng+gappa) and confidence-aware classification (IDTAXA)**, which abstain on novel ASVs instead of force-assigning to an embedding-indistinguishable congener. Crucially, **no published DNA embedding has been shown to beat alignment at fine 16S identity ordering** — the one paper claiming embeddings match VSEARCH/BLAST tests taxonomy *classification*, not congener %identity *ranking* — so the durable architecture is embeddings for recall, alignment (or alignment-distilled scorers) for exact order.

## 2. Framework landscape

### 2a. Better embeddings — most will NOT fix fine ordering

Bigger or newer *general* DNA foundation models will not fix this, because none is trained so that embedding distance tracks nucleotide identity, and head-to-head benchmarking (Feng et al., "Benchmarking DNA foundation models," PMC12663285, 2025) of DNABERT-2/NT-v2/HyenaDNA/Caduceus/GROVER finds **no model dominates and zero-shot embeddings are task-dependent** — scaling alone buys no discrimination. Specifically:

- **NT-v2-2.5B / Evo2 / HyenaDNA / Caduceus / GROVER**: same objective-mismatch as your 500M model; NT-2.5B costs ~5× the inference for no expected ordering gain. Evo2 is a *generative* long-context design model, not a metric encoder. HyenaDNA/Caduceus give single-nucleotide tokenization (good in principle for SNP signal) but, untuned, optimize a generative objective. **Use these only as backbones to fine-tune (§2c), not as drop-in re-rankers.**
- **DNABERT-S** (arXiv:2402.08777, ISMB 2025): the most relevant *idea* — explicitly contrastive and species-aware (Weighted SimCLR + Manifold Instance Mixup + Curriculum Contrastive Learning, 768-d, doubles species-clustering ARI). **But it is trained on 10 kb chunks and evaluated only down to 2 kb — it is untested/out-of-distribution at your 373 bp amplicon length, and it provides no congeneric/near-identical evaluation** (the verification corrects a common overstatement: the paper does *not* show a "collapse below 256 bp" — that regime is simply never tested). It targets *species* separation, not *intra-genus/strain* ordering, which is exactly where your cloud lives. Verdict: do not use the off-the-shelf checkpoint as a re-ranker; reuse its *training recipe* (§2c).
- **BarcodeBERT** (Arias et al., *Bioinformatics Advances* 2025; arXiv:2311.02401): proof-of-concept that a small purpose-built marker-gene transformer can *match BLAST species accuracy at ~55× speed*. But it is masked-LM (not contrastive) and scoped to **COI ~658 bp invertebrate barcodes — not 16S**; the published model does not transfer. Its value is as an architecture/training template; its successor **BarcodeMamba** (arXiv:2412.11084) does the same job more parameter-efficiently with a state-space backbone.
- **Tokenizer caveat**: DNABERT-2's BPE collapses ~373 bp into ~15-70 tokens, losing per-base signal the authors themselves flag as a short-sequence weakness. For V4-V5, prefer **single-nucleotide / short-k models** (NT at 6-mer, HyenaDNA/Caduceus character-level) as fine-tuning backbones.

### 2b. Sequence-native (alignment) — the exact tool for *this* failure

"%identity" is *defined by an alignment*; your ground truth already is one. So the correct re-ranker is the same operation computed for more candidates, not a different embedding.

- **VSEARCH `usearch_global`** computes the *optimal* global alignment via 8-way 16-bit SIMD full dynamic programming (Needleman-Wunsch-style), explicitly **not** the seed-and-extend heuristic of USEARCH/BLAST, and is documented as **more accurate than USEARCH** (Rognes et al. 2016, PeerJ e2584). Short 16S amplicons are exactly the regime where the full-DP cost is well compensated.
- **The accuracy knob**: VSEARCH/USEARCH are heuristic *by default* (stop after `maxaccepts=1` / `maxrejects=32`), which in a dense cloud can miss the true best hit. **Set `--maxaccepts 0 --maxrejects 0` → "the complete database is searched"** (verified, VSEARCH manual), giving exact %identity ordering that reproduces your Biopython ground truth rather than approximating it. Use global alignment with terminal-gap handling for a short query vs longer reference (`usearch_global` does this natively).
- **Throughput**: multithreaded against ~1e5 short refs, tens of ms to low-seconds per query even with both limits at 0, because the k-mer prefilter prunes the DP candidate set while the *final* ranking stays exact. A whole study (3-4k ASVs) finishes in single-digit minutes on a laptop.
- **MMseqs2** (`--search-type 3`): 100-10,000× faster than BLAST at comparable sensitivity for proteins (Steinegger & Söding 2017, nbt.3988). Caveats from verification: there is **no 16S/SSU-specific MMseqs2 workflow** (the taxonomy workflow is the general 2bLCA, and it **drops to top-hit mode for nucleotide-vs-nucleotide** input); its long k-mer seeds were tuned for proteins/longer nucleotides, so for 373 bp at high identity you must raise sensitivity (`-s 7.5`) and verify it doesn't lose the marginal best hit. The 2025 GPU MMseqs2 paper (Nat Methods s41592-025-02819-8) is **protein-focused and "6× vs CPU MMseqs2," not "6× vs BLAST."** VSEARCH's full-DP final pass is safer for exact ordering.
- **BLAST+ `blastn`**: correct but slowest indexed aligner and *also heuristic* — not more accurate than exhaustive VSEARCH for ordering. **minimap2**: optimized for ≥1 kb long reads/assemblies — not recommended for high-identity short-amplicon best-hit ranking.
- **Skip for closest-hit**: **sourmash FracMinHash / Mash** (limited sensitivity when k-mer cardinality ≈ sketch size — gene-/amplicon-sized queries are the documented failure case); **Kraken2 / Centrifuge / CLARK** (exact-k-mer LCA classifiers that **collapse to the lowest common ancestor by design** — genus/family, not a single closest genome). These serve the taxonomy facet only.

### 2c. Contrastive / metric-learning fine-tuning — highest-leverage if you keep a single-vector retriever

This is the only option that *trains the metric on the target ordering*, and you already hold the exact label it should reproduce (Biopython %identity over your fixed 97,624-insert DB).

- **Scorpio** (Refahi, Rosen et al., *Communications Biology* 2025, s42003-025-07902-6; github.com/MsAlEhR/Scorpio): a **triplet-network contrastive framework for nucleotide sequences** on genomic-LM + k-mer embeddings, explicitly built so embedding distance reflects **hierarchical taxonomic structure**, with confirmed generalization to **novel taxa**. The closest published template to what you'd build.
- **The decisive customization — distill alignment %identity directly**: (i) mine **hard negatives = con-generic references** (the embedding-indistinguishable cloud, at strain/intra-genus granularity — DNABERT-S's hard-negative insight applied at your resolution); (ii) use a **rank/regression loss on pairwise %identity** so cosine becomes monotonic in alignment identity — this optimizes your headline Spearman metric directly; (iii) backbone = single-nt/small-k model fine-tuned at native amplicon length, not a 10 kb-pretrained checkpoint used out of distribution.
- **Cost/plausibility**: highest effort of the three, but data-generation cost is largely sunk (you have the alignment ground truth and a fixed DB), and fine-tuning ≤~120M params is days on one GPU. Honest caveat: given V4-V5's biological information ceiling, this likely still won't reach *exact* ordering — so it is a complement to, not a replacement for, an alignment/Identity reranker.

### 2d. Learned %identity scorers and late-interaction — fast middle grounds

- **Identity / FASTCAR** (Girgis lab, *NAR Genomics & Bioinformatics* 2021, lqab001): converts sequences to k-mer histograms, computes 26 alignment-free pair statistics, fits a self-supervised GLM. Reports **0.97 Pearson with Needleman-Wunsch identity**, is 2-80× faster than BLAST/Mash/USEARCH, and was run *at 16S scale* (573 billion 16S comparisons in 13.5 h). Verification caveat: the 0.97 was measured on the general P27 dataset (68 seqs), **not on 16S congeners specifically** — the 16S claim is throughput, not the 0.97 accuracy. With 1.6-4.7% MAE it is a *reranker*, not a final call: ideal as cosine → Identity-rerank top-500 → exact-align top-10-20.
- **Late interaction (ColBERT-style MaxSim)**: keep *per-token* (per-k-mer) embeddings instead of mean-pooling; score a pair by summing, over query tokens, the **max** similarity to any reference token — sensitive to *which positions* match, far cheaper than a cross-encoder, more expressive than single-vector cosine. The biological analogue is **PLMSearch** (Liu et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2024): protein-LM embedding retrieve → alignment-based PLMAlign rerank — exactly your target architecture. Caveat: **no off-the-shelf nucleotide ColBERT exists**; it would require training with %identity supervision (build-it option).
- **Dense + sparse fusion at the candidate stage**: your root cause is the textbook case for hybrid retrieval. Add a sparse signal sensitive to exact substitutions (k-mer BM25, k≈8-12; or MinHash/FracMinHash Jaccard with a *whole/large* sketch on these short inserts), and fuse with **Reciprocal Rank Fusion** (RRF, k≈60) — scale-free, robust, typically lifts recall 15-30% and often pre-corrects ordering. Use the sparse signal for candidate generation/fusion only, *not* as the final identity estimate (FracMinHash→ANI is calibrated for genomes, not single short genes).
- **ANN indexing**: at 97,624 refs your brute-force cosine (0.06 ms/query) needs no index. If you scale, **FAISS HNSW** (M=48, ef=100, ~1 ms at ≥95% recall) or **GSearch** (k-mer hashing + HNSW, NAR 2024 e74; classifies 8,000 queries against millions of genomes in minutes, with built-in 16S k-NN vote).

### 2e. Taxonomy & phylogenetic placement — for the call/novelty deliverable

When the deliverable is a *taxon* or a *novelty flag* rather than a single genome, stop trying to perfectly order near-identical references and ask where on the reference tree the ASV belongs.

- **DEPP / C-DEPP** (Jiang et al., *Syst. Biol.* 2023, 72(1):17; *Bioinformatics* 2024, btae361) — **the best bridge to your existing embedding stack**: it trains a CNN so that *embedding distance ≈ phylogenetic tree distance*, i.e. your "embed + nearest-neighbor" idea done right. Verified numbers: on **100 bp V4 it averages 3.4 edges of error vs EPA-ng 4.12 and APPLES+JC 4.94** — a learned embedding *beating* alignment-based ML placement on short V4 — and it is far better calibrated (only **0.2% of wrong placements get max support vs 62% for EPA-ng**), directly addressing over-confidence. **C-DEPP** scaled this to Greengenes2 (331,270 species; 20M V4 ASVs in 41 h). Note this beats alignment on *placement error*, not on exact %identity ordering.
- **EPA-ng + gappa** (Barbera et al., *Syst. Biol.* 2019; Czech et al., *Bioinformatics* 2020): the standard ML placement + post-processing stack — gappa turns a jplace file into an LWR-weighted taxonomy call with EDPL-based uncertainty/novelty. **SEPP / q2-fragment-insertion** (Janssen et al., *mSystems* 2018): exact-ASV insertion beats de novo (P<10⁻³²), **85% of unambiguous V4 fragments place within 0.037 branch-length (below species), retains all sequences whereas closed-ref similarity rejected 35%** — the directly relevant short-fragment benchmark.
- **IDTAXA / DECIPHER** (Murali et al., *Microbiome* 2018): best-in-class for your **over-classification/novelty** concern — it **abstains on novel labels** instead of force-assigning to a con-generic reference, and beats BLAST/SINTAX/RDP. The targeted fix for not falsely assigning a novel ASV.
- **Sobering ceiling**: Bokulich's "Beating Naive Bayes" (*Front. Microbiol.* 2021) found random forests and CNNs **none beat naive Bayes** at 16S; residual error (~genus F 0.866 near the ~0.89 max) is **biological information limit of the short region**, not algorithmic — mirroring your own diminishing-returns-with-k finding.
- **Head-to-head** (Ewers/Czech/Mahé/Dunthorn, bioRxiv 2022): EPA-ng+gappa vs VSEARCH **agree at high similarity and diverge precisely at low similarity** (novel OTUs), where placement is more conservative/robust. Decision rule: **similarity/cosine for the high-identity core, placement (or IDTAXA abstention) for the low-identity novel tail.**
- **Reference DB**: prefer **GTDB** (genome-anchored, ~96%/90% labeled to genus/species) since your references are genome-excised inserts; **Greengenes2** (built *by DEPP-placing 20M V4 ASVs into a genome tree* — the productized version of your task) is the natural backbone if you adopt placement. Curate reproducibly with **RESCRIPt**.

## 4. What to prototype next (ranked by expected gain per effort)

1. **Alignment-rerank of the cosine top-k (do this first, regardless of everything else).** Take your existing cosine top-200-500 and rerank with `vsearch --usearch_global queries.fa --db refs.fa --id 0.97 --maxaccepts 0 --maxrejects 0 --top_hits_only` (or Biopython over the shortlist). **Effort: hours.** Expected gain: the largest and most certain — top-1/top-5 become exact *within the ~92% recall ceiling*, directly closing the 54.7%→~100% and 37-47%→~90%+ gaps. This is the highest-confidence item in the whole report and is essentially free. Measure recall@k of your retriever (currently ~92% @100) — that becomes your new ceiling; widen k or add RRF fusion to raise it.

2. **Add a k-mer-sparse signal + RRF fusion to the retriever, and/or drop in Identity/FASTCAR as a fast pre-reranker.** Build k-mer BM25 (k≈8-12) or a whole-sketch MinHash list, fuse with cosine via RRF, then rerank the fused top-500 with Identity before the exact-align pass. **Effort: 1-2 days.** Expected gain: lifts the ~92% recall ceiling (the thing alignment-rerank can't fix) and gives near-alignment ordering at a fraction of alignment cost — the right move if aligning hundreds of candidates per ASV across studies becomes a bottleneck.

3. **For the taxonomy/novelty deliverable, stand up DEPP/C-DEPP placement (or EPA-ng+gappa) on GTDB/Greengenes2, plus IDTAXA for calibrated per-ASV calls.** **Effort: 2-4 days** (C-DEPP has a Greengenes2 model; IDTAXA is a DECIPHER one-liner). Expected gain: high for the *novel/low-identity tail* and for honest, rank-appropriate, well-calibrated calls — DEPP beats EPA-ng on 100 bp V4 (3.4 vs 4.12 edges) and is dramatically better calibrated (0.2% vs 62% over-confident wrong placements). This is the natural upgrade of your embedding idea: an embedding *trained to recover tree distance* rather than a generic one that happens to correlate 0.55 with %identity.

**Lower priority / higher effort — contrastive fine-tuning (§2c):** only if items 1-2 leave a residual ordering gap you must close inside a single-vector retriever. It is the only approach that trains the metric on your exact target, but it is days-to-weeks of work and, given V4-V5's biological resolution ceiling, is unlikely to beat simply aligning the shortlist. Treat it as research, not the production fix.

**Honest bottom line on what's proven vs speculative:** *Proven* — alignment is the exact %identity operation and VSEARCH computes it correctly and fast at your scale; cosine recalls ~92% so a reranker is sufficient; DEPP beats alignment-based placement on short V4 and is better calibrated; IDTAXA abstains on novelty. *Speculative* — that any contrastive/embedding model can beat alignment at *fine %identity ordering* of congeners (no published result shows this; the one paper claiming embeddings rival VSEARCH/BLAST measures taxonomy *classification*, not congener *ranking*). Bet on the proven retrieve-then-align architecture; treat learned-metric embeddings as an optional accelerator, not the accuracy source.

Three claims need correction before publication: (1) DNABERT-S "collapses below 256 bp per the paper" → restate as untested/out-of-distribution at amplicon length; (2) MMseqs2 GPU "6× faster than BLAST single-batch" → it is 6× vs CPU MMseqs2 and is protein-focused; cite Steinegger & Söding 2017 for the BLAST comparison; (3) MMseqs2 has no 16S/SSU-specific workflow and drops to top-hit mode for nucleotide-vs-nucleotide. Two need hedging: FASTCAR's 0.97 Pearson was measured on P27 (68 seqs), not 16S congeners; BarcodeBERT's exact 29M/4-layer architecture spec is unverified though its scope and BLAST-parity/55× are confirmed.
